# Reading a low click rate

A low CTR next to search is expected for some surfaces and alarming for others.
The mistake is treating the number as a verdict before asking what the click
means on this surface.

## Why the comparison to search misleads

Search shows an ad to someone who typed what they want. A conversational surface
may show an ad inside an answer the person was already reading, with the
placement matched to the topic, not to a purchase request. A lower click rate
can simply mean fewer people are ready to leave. That is different from the ad
being bad.

So **do not put the CTR beside a search CTR and call the gap a failure.** Do not
call it a success either. It is an input.

## The clicks that did happen are the evidence

When clicks are scarce, read what the scarce clicks did:

- **Share of visitors new to you.** If most are people you could not have reached
  through your existing channels, the surface may be adding customers rather
  than harvesting ones you already had. Check this on the backend, not only the
  platform report.
- **Conversion rate per click** and the depth of the visit, against your other
  traffic on the same page.
- **Cost per acquired customer,** which is the only number that combines the
  above with what the surface charged.

Low volume with a good outcome cost is a small, working channel. High volume
with a poor outcome cost is an expensive one.

## Two traps

**New-visitor share can flatter a tiny sample.** Ten clicks of which nine are
new is a headline, not a finding. Report the count beside the share.

**New does not mean valuable.** A new visitor who never buys is just a cheap
click. Follow new visitors to the outcome before crediting the surface.

## Separate three causes of a low rate

Before concluding the surface underperforms, rule out the other readings:

1. **The surface works this way.** Normal for the format, and the quality read
   above is what matters.
2. **The ad is weak.** Test the creative against another creative on the same
   surface. Do not test it against another surface.
3. **The placement or topic match is off.** If the ad is appearing next to
   content that does not suit the offer, the click rate reflects relevance,
   not appetite. Check where it is being shown, where the platform exposes that.

Only the second is fixed by changing the ad.

## Output

The CTR and the click count it is based on. The quality read: new-customer share
with its count, conversion per click against your other traffic, and cost per
acquired customer. Which of the three causes the evidence supports. A verdict
using `no-benchmark-verdict`.

## What this does not cover

Working out whether the cost per click is affordable, see `cpm-to-cpa-ceiling`.
Proving the visitors would not have arrived anyway, which needs a holdout test
rather than a platform report.

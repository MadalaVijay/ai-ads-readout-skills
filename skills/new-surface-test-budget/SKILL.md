# Sizing a first test on a new surface

A test that is too small proves nothing, and one that is too big is a bet. The
job is to buy **just enough results to make a decision, then stop and decide.**

## Start from the decision

Before choosing a budget, finish this sentence: "If the outcome cost is below X
after N results I will scale, and if it is above Y I will stop." X and Y come
from your own economics, see `cpm-to-cpa-ceiling`. N is the number of results
needed to believe the cost.

Spend is then the cost per result you expect, multiplied by N, plus a margin
because the first estimate is usually wrong. Work this out on paper, using a
range for the expected cost, and look at the worst case. If the worst case is
more than you would be comfortable losing, shrink the question, not the
discipline.

## Choose N honestly

Very small counts are noise. Below roughly ten results, one lucky or unlucky
week decides the verdict. Treat ten as a floor and tune it: larger when the
decision is expensive to reverse, smaller when it is cheap.

If the budget you can afford cannot buy N results, say so before starting. A test
that cannot reach a verdict is not a cheaper test, it is a wasted one.

## Ring-fence the money

Fund the test from a separate line, not by quietly drawing down a proven channel.
Two reasons:

- A test that is allowed to cannibalise your best channel makes the new surface
  look better than it is, because it takes credit for conversions that would have
  happened anyway.
- If it fails, you will want to know exactly what the lesson cost.

## Change one thing at a time

A first test that varies the audience, the creative, the landing page and the
objective together cannot say which one worked. Fix everything you can and vary
one thing. If you cannot afford that, accept a weaker conclusion and say so.

## Set the stop rule in advance

Write down the date and the spend or result count at which you will decide. When
it arrives, decide. Common ways this fails:

- Extending "one more week" because the numbers were close.
- Reading the first few days, which are often unrepresentative because delivery
  is still settling.
- Moving the target after seeing the result.

## Do not scale on the first good week

A good first week on a small sample often regresses. Require the result count
from your plan and a second period holding the same direction before raising
spend. When you do scale, raise in steps and watch the outcome cost, because a
surface that worked at a small budget may reach worse people at a larger one.

## Output

The decision sentence with X, Y and N. The spend needed with a range and the
worst case. Where the money comes from. What is being varied and what is fixed.
The stop date and trigger. The condition for scaling and the size of the first
step.

## What this does not cover

General experiment design, power calculations and statistical testing. This is a
budget-and-discipline skill for a first test on a new surface, not a statistics
course.
